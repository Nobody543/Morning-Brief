UK Politics

Andy Burnham outlines devolution and defence agenda in first Commons speech

Prime Minister Andy Burnham made his first Parliamentary appearance more than six weeks into his tenure, setting out an ambitious agenda spanning devolution, essential service reform, and defence spending. He blamed decades of policy mistakes—particularly Brexit and Margaret Thatcher's legacy—for prolonged economic stagnation, pledging to restore optimism and build momentum over the next decade.

On defence, Burnham committed to increasing spending to 3% of GDP by 2030, a significant escalation, with Chancellor John Healey's appointment explicitly chosen to reflect the government's resolve on military investment. He signaled openness to electoral reform if consensus emerges within the next Parliament, and took a pragmatic stance on green energy, leaving room for approvals like the Jackdaw gasfield while pursuing renewables. He also flagged examination of water industry renationalization.

Tory leader Kemi Badenoch criticized the proposals as offering "more government control, more taxes and more politicians," framing the speech as an expansion of state intervention rather than economic reform.

UK signals policy reset on Israel; Tel Aviv warns of retaliation for sanctions

Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband announced a "comprehensive reset" of UK policy toward Israel, pledging measures to prevent British companies from participating in West Bank settlements, specifically targeting the E1 development plan—a controversial expansion that critics argue would bisect Palestinian territory. Israel responded with a warning that it would retaliate against any British sanctions, signaling deep friction over the policy shift. The announcement reflects the new government's intention to take a more assertive stance on the Israeli-Palestinian conflict, though the specific form of any UK response remains under formulation.

UK borrowing costs hit 28-year high ahead of October Budget

Long-term UK borrowing costs have reached their highest level since 1998, adding significant fiscal pressure on Prime Minister Burnham ahead of his first Budget announcement. The surge will increase the government's debt servicing costs and constrain options for addressing social and economic priorities.

Government narrows early prison release scheme following backlash

After public outcry over the release of serious offenders, Prime Minister Burnham has modified the early prison release scheme to exclude serious offences including manslaughter, murder, and grooming gang crimes. The changes address an overcrowding crisis while responding to victim concerns, specifically blocking release of perpetrators of notorious cases like the killing of police officer Andrew Harper.

Illegal tobacco and vape enforcement struggles with low closure rates

Centre for Social Justice is a centre-right think tank

A Centre for Social Justice report finds that over 4,000 shops were raided in 2025 for selling illicit tobacco, cigarettes, and vapes, yet fewer than one in ten were permanently closed. The report highlights the sustained enforcement challenge against illegal retailers on Britain's high streets, with a cited example of a Manchester operation that closed 206 criminal establishments. The centre-right think tank's analysis underscores the difficulty of translating enforcement action into lasting market removal.

Report: Dutch employment policies could cut youth joblessness by 670,000

Centre for Social Justice is a centre-right think tank

A Centre for Social Justice report argues that adopting Dutch-style employment policies could address Britain's NEET (Not in Education, Employment, or Training) crisis, potentially reducing the number of young people outside the workforce by 670,000 and generating £15 billion in economic benefit. The analysis frames this as a policy comparison between UK and Netherlands approaches to youth employment, suggesting significant upside from regulatory and structural reforms.
