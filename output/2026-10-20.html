Burnham moves to protect motorists from 'rip-off' private parking fines with new code of practice

Prime Minister Andy Burnham has announced new rules for private car parks in England, Scotland and Wales designed to crack down on excessive parking charges. Under the new official code of practice, drivers will be given five minutes to decide whether to park and ten minutes to leave after their session ends before facing penalties. The measures are framed as part of a broader "everyday fixes" agenda to ease the cost-of-living burden on consumers.

Burnham instructs officials to develop 'radical options' for crisis-hit Thames Water

Prime Minister Andy Burnham has instructed government officials to discard existing plans for Thames Water and develop more radical approaches to address the struggling utility's crisis, in an early test of his pledge to bring essential services under public control. Thames Water—which serves over a quarter of England's population—has been weighed down by over £20 billion in debt accumulated through previous ownership structures and shareholder dividend policies while infrastructure has deteriorated. The company requested an additional £2.4 billion from creditors recently. Officials are being told to evaluate all options without constraint from the previous administration's approach, potentially including converting environmental fines into government equity stakes. The utility has faced record environmental fines for sewage pollution and infrastructure failures.

Northern Ireland secretary rejects claims Troubles legacy bill amounts to amnesty for soldiers

As amended legislation returns to the House of Commons, the Northern Ireland Secretary has defended the revised Troubles legacy bill against criticism that it protects former soldiers from prosecution. The bill—which replaces the 2023 Legacy Act—removes a controversial conditional immunity scheme that offered amnesty to both paramilitaries and British security forces who cooperated with inquiries. The new version requires formal consent from the UK Attorney General before any serious Troubles-related prosecution can proceed. The change is intended to establish "no equivalence between crown forces and paramilitaries," though victims' groups and human rights organizations have criticized the bill as still placing insufficient focus on victim rights and accountability.

Trump administration blocks transfer of Patriot interceptor missiles to Ukraine

The Trump administration has not approved the transfer of Patriot air-defense interceptor missiles to Ukraine despite the country's critical shortage—Ukrainian officials report only a handful remain in stockpile. At least some of the missiles are being transferred from allied nations, requiring approval from US Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, but he has neither formally rejected nor completed the steps needed to authorize the handoff. Ukrainian President Zelensky has noted that allies have already paid for the weapons through PURL, a NATO-coordinated mechanism for funding US-made military aid to Ukraine. The delay comes amid heightened tensions between Trump and Zelensky, with Zelensky recently suggesting Ukraine could reduce attacks on Russian refineries in exchange for Patriot support. Patriot systems are surface-to-air missile platforms essential for air defense against Russian aircraft and missile strikes.

Egypt's parliament set to vote on military support for Saudi Arabia in Houthi conflict

Egypt is moving toward direct military involvement in the broader Middle Eastern conflict, with President Abdel Fattah al-Sisi requesting parliamentary approval to authorize armed forces to defend Saudi Arabia against attacks by Yemen's Houthis if Riyadh requests assistance. The decision reflects both economic and security pressures: Saudi Arabia maintains a significant financial stake in Egypt's economy, and Egypt depends on Suez Canal fees from Red Sea shipping that has been disrupted by Houthi attacks. A drone strike on Egyptian vessels in Damietta in July demonstrated Cairo cannot remain isolated from the conflict. The move signals an escalation from Egypt's previous measured approach, though it stops short of the joint defense agreement Turkey and Pakistan signed with Saudi Arabia in August. Egypt possesses one of the Arab world's largest armed forces, with fighter jets including US-made F-16s and French Rafales, and has prior experience with regional military operations including the 2015 Saudi-led intervention in Yemen.
