UK Deemed 'Dangerously' Reliant on China for Critical Minerals

A parliamentary investigation by the business select committee has found the UK faces serious strategic vulnerabilities due to its dependence on China for critical raw materials essential to defence, renewable energy infrastructure, and AI development. The committee is calling for an urgent centralised policy and a new 'critical minerals finance facility' with grants, equity, and debt funding to accelerate diversification of supply sources and reduce this dependency. The MPs warn that without action, net zero targets are also at risk.

Hong Kong Activist Joshua Wong Sentenced to 10 Years in Second National Security Conviction

Hong Kong pro-democracy activist Joshua Wong was sentenced to 10 years in prison on November 4, 2026, for his second conviction under a Beijing-imposed national security law. The sentence—one of the heaviest under the law, matching that given to former law professor Benny Tai and surpassed only by former media tycoon Jimmy Lai's 20-year sentence—comes just two months before Wong was due to be freed from a previous conviction under the same law. The prosecution charged Wong with conspiracy to collude with foreign forces, citing his advocacy work abroad, including lobbying efforts and coordination with overseas-based activists.

Wong, who came to prominence in 2012 as a high school student protesting national education and later co-founded the political party Demosisto, has become a target because Beijing views him as an anti-China, pro-independence figure crossing its red lines. The Beijing-imposed national security law—which criminalizes secession, subversion, foreign collusion, and terrorism—has been used to prosecute Wong twice and 165 others in total, with only two acquittals recorded among all those charged.

Critics view the successive prosecutions as evidence that national security charges are being repeatedly weaponized to extend incarceration of political prisoners, as part of a broader crackdown on Hong Kong's once-thriving pro-democracy movement that intensified after Beijing imposed the national security law in 2020. Hong Kong authorities maintain the law is essential for stability following the 2019 pro-democracy protests.

Why Modern Wars Are Becoming Harder to End

A roundtable of five national security experts examines the structural reasons armed conflicts are increasingly difficult to resolve decisively. According to former US Assistant Secretary of State Dafna H. Rand, wars in the 21st century no longer typically end in clear peace settlements or total victory; instead they settle into recurring pauses or frozen conflicts. She notes that the US finds it difficult to shape war outcomes even when providing crucial military and financial support to allies—particularly when Washington and its partners disagree on political goals or what victory means, public support wanes, and interests diverge over time. Additional perspectives explore factors like greater global economic interdependence allowing outside powers to sustain losing belligerents.
