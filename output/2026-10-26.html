UK government to double rough sleeping funding to nearly £900m ahead of Christmas target

Prime Minister Andy Burnham is doubling government spending on ending rough sleeping to nearly £900m by the end of the current parliament, with Downing Street allocating a further £432m in this week's Budget. Burnham made ending rough sleeping his first pledge upon entering office in July 2026 and has committed to offering every rough sleeper in England a safe route off the streets or wider support by Christmas as a central aim of his premiership.

Cabinet rift over Rosebank oilfield as Chancellor backs North Sea project while Energy Secretary leans toward rejection

A cabinet split has emerged over the Rosebank oilfield, Britain's largest untapped oil reserve located northwest of Shetland. Chancellor John Healey backs the controversial North Sea project, while Energy Secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh is reportedly leaning toward rejection following her approval of the smaller Jackdaw gasfield. Fahnbulleh, who holds formal decision-making authority, has distinguished between the two projects: she approved Jackdaw because the UK needs domestic gas for its transition to net zero, but argues Rosebank—which would emit roughly seven times more carbon and produce oil mainly for export—does not serve the same energy security rationale. The decision has exposed deep divisions within Labour, with environmental campaigners backing the likely rejection while industry and some MPs urge approval.

PM Burnham's call for EU free movement debate backed by senior cabinet, but raises alarm among 'red wall' MPs

Prime Minister Andy Burnham has sparked a party divide by calling for an "honest discussion" about restoring free movement with Europe, making clear it would no longer be a red line in EU negotiations. The proposal has been backed by senior cabinet ministers including Angela Rayner and Ed Miliband and by business leaders, but has risked a rift with Labour MPs representing Brexit-voting "red wall" constituencies, who warn it could jeopardise their seats. The move signals the government's willingness to reconsider a core Brexit position, though the political sensitivity remains acute given the electoral geography Labour holds.
