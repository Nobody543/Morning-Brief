Bank of England raises interest rates to 4% for first time in three years

Opposing viewpoints included; criticism emphasises household impact

The Bank of England raised its base rate by 0.25 percentage points to 4%, marking the first increase in over three years from the previous 3.75% level—a move most analysts had anticipated. The decision has drawn criticism, however. The Guardian has expressed concern about the timing amid an ongoing cost of living crisis, emphasising the perspective of households facing hardship as borrowing costs rise.

Burnham launches structured dialogue on slavery apology, ruling out reparations

Prime Minister Andy Burnham has asked Commonwealth nations affected by Britain's role in slavery to help shape a formal apology through a "structured dialogue" during the Commonwealth Heads of Government Meeting in Antigua and Barbuda. Though the plans were received positively, the British government continues to reject calls for financial reparations, which campaigners have pursued for decades. The UK is watching closely the cautionary experience of the Netherlands, which began a similar apology process five years ago—many felt that effort was mishandled and exclusionary, mirroring past inequality—as it attempts a historic shift in how it addresses its colonial past.
