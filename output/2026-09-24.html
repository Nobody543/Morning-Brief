UK to review Chagos Islands deal after Trump criticism undermines US support

Defence Secretary Wes Streeting has confirmed the UK will review its Chagos Islands agreement with Mauritius, following Donald Trump's description of the deal as "terrible" and his statement that the US would not support it. Trump made the remarks to Prime Minister Andy Burnham. The original deal, signed by the previous government, would have handed sovereignty of the archipelago to Mauritius, ending a long-running territorial dispute. Trump's opposition and the withdrawal of US backing has forced a reconsideration of the agreement.

UK losing up to £6.5bn annually in EU trade due to unaligned product testing standards

The UK is forgoing substantial annual exports to the EU because successive governments have failed to secure a mutual recognition agreement that would eliminate duplicate product testing requirements. Research by the IPPR thinktank estimates the loss at around £6.5bn per year—equivalent to 0.18% of national income. Motor vehicle and parts exports would be £2.48bn–£3.42bn higher annually; electronics could add £1.17bn–£1.67bn; pharmaceuticals an estimated £740m–£820m. Many companies have abandoned EU exports or relocated subsidiaries within the trade bloc to avoid the administrative burden of meeting differing standards.

UK Conservatives propose slashing universal credit for inadequate national insurance contributions

The Conservative Party has announced a plan to cut universal credit payments by a third for claimants who have not paid sufficient national insurance contributions, restricting the reduced "subsistence allowance" to a dedicated debit card that would prevent purchases of alcohol and tobacco. Charities have condemned the proposal as a "fast track to increased poverty," citing ongoing impacts of austerity cuts and the cost-of-living crisis on families. The model is based on an Australian cashless debit card system that was eventually scrapped after trials found limited evidence it changed recipient behavior.

England to introduce vocational GCSEs alongside traditional subjects in education overhaul

The UK government is implementing its biggest GCSE qualification changes in over a decade, introducing vocational courses starting in 2029 to address a youth employment crisis. From 2029, pupils will be able to supplement compulsory GCSE subjects—English, maths, and sciences—with new vocational options including construction, creative media production, and child development. The overhaul is framed as a response to nearly a million young people currently out of work or training, and is part of the government's strategy to offer educational pathways suited to diverse student needs.

Sam Altman tells UN Security Council: AI must remain under human control to avoid catastrophe

OpenAI CEO Sam Altman addressed the UN Security Council, framing AI as having the potential for either a positive "Renaissance of creativity and discovery" or a destructive "Industrial Revolution of upheaval." He emphasized that powerful AI systems must be kept under human control, warning that letting systems advance faster than institutions can manage them poses severe risks. Altman identified two key dangers: first, losing control entirely as AI moves too fast to follow or intervene; second, undue concentration of power in too few hands. He stressed that companies must not accept excessive technological risk for competitive advantage, and that AI models should not be developed if they cannot be reliably kept under human control, noting that catastrophe risks at any level—whether 10%, 1%, or 0.1%—are unacceptable. Altman positioned human agency and purpose at the centre of beneficial AI: systems should expand people's capabilities to learn, create, and build rather than automating human purpose away.
