UK Politics

Burnham launches 'Your First Home' scheme to help first-time buyers without family financial backing

Prime Minister Andy Burnham has announced a new help-to-buy programme called 'Your First Home' designed to help first-time buyers in England who have regular income but lack savings for a large deposit or family financial support. The initiative specifically targets those without access to the 'bank of mum and dad,' addressing a structural barrier to home ownership for younger generations without inherited wealth.

Burnham: radical change possible despite economic headwinds, fiscal stability crucial before tackling bigger problems

On the eve of Labour conference, Prime Minister Andy Burnham told the Guardian that despite challenging public finances ahead of next month's budget, he can still deliver on promises of radical change for the country. He emphasised that achieving fiscal stability is a prerequisite for being able to focus on larger policy reforms, though he indicated room for targeted action even under tight constraints.

Prime Minister pledges £210m for high street regeneration fund

Prime Minister Burnham has announced a £210 million fund to support communities in transforming derelict buildings and keeping local businesses open on high streets across the UK, part of broader regeneration efforts.

Rayner criticises Home Office visa reforms for care workers as 'disruptive' and moving the goalposts

Housing Secretary Angela Rayner has renewed her criticism of Home Office reforms that would require low-paid migrant workers in non-graduate roles to spend 15 years in the UK before qualifying for permanent settlement. She has described the policy as 'disruptive' and as moving the goalposts, having previously attacked it as 'un-British,' signalling ongoing internal Labour government tension over immigration policy.

Burnham reaches out to farmers to rebuild trust after Starmer-era tax changes

Prime Minister Andy Burnham is attempting to rebuild relations with the farming community following tax changes introduced under his predecessor Keir Starmer that damaged rural relations. The Guardian reports ministers have been engaging with farming activists, with Burnham himself contacting prominent farmers to build bridges.

Milburn review recommends fast-track disability benefits access for severely disabled young people

A government review led by former Labour health secretary Alan Milburn will recommend that severely disabled young people aged 16-24 with complex lifelong needs should receive fast-track, unconditional access to disability benefits rather than having to repeatedly prove they cannot work, removing the burden of ongoing assessments for those with little prospect of employment.

Glasgow City Council pauses fire-and-rehire plan for 23,000 staff

Glasgow City Council has paused its plan to dismiss and rehire 23,000 staff members. Talks between the council and three unions will resume early next week in an attempt to reach a new pay deal and avoid the widespread redundancies.

Northern Ireland court overturns Orange Order march ban after nearly 30 years, allowing loyalist parade down Garvaghy Road

A court has overturned a ban and permitted an Orange Order loyalist parade to proceed down Garvaghy Road in Portadown, Northern Ireland, for the first time in nearly 30 years. The march is scheduled for Sunday morning and has raised concerns about potential sectarian violence, following a legal back-and-forth in recent days. The Orange Order represents Protestant/unionist traditions; Garvaghy Road is a nationalist area, making the route historically contentious in Northern Ireland's divided communities.

Government accelerates work on laws to ban concert ticket touts

The government is stepping up efforts to deliver legislation outlawing for-profit resale of concert tickets on platforms like Viagogo and StubHub, a pledge made in Labour's 2024 election manifesto. Artists including Tinie Tempah and Mike Joyce (drummer from The Smiths) have increased pressure on the government to deliver on the commitment to stop fans being exploited by ticket resellers.

China and US establish AI safety channel and agree to tariff cuts, marking de-escalation in tensions

The countries have also agreed to maintain ongoing trade and military negotiations, reflecting a diplomatic effort to manage tensions and establish guardrails around emerging technology competition.
