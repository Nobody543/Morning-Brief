Institute for Government questions whether civil service cuts can hit targets

The Institute for Government has questioned whether Chancellor Healey's ambitious plan to cut civil service administration budgets by 20% by 2029/30 can be achieved, noting the civil service failed to meet previous targets. The think tank identifies that voluntary exit schemes are insufficient—civil service exits actually declined from 38,610 in 2024/25 to 37,945 in 2025/26. Tighter devolution of powers and more predictable compulsory redundancy rounds every two to three years would be needed to hit targets. The analysis also warns that Healey's plan to fund a new £50m "transformation fund" and AI organization called "GovAI" relies on savings that are "currently more imagined than real," and argues the government must focus AI adoption on achieving policy outcomes rather than adopting technology for its own sake.
