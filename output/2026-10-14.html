UK Politics

Burnham hosts Zelenskyy in Downing Street display of UK support amid Trump tensions

Prime Minister Andy Burnham is hosting Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy at Downing Street on Wednesday in a symbolic show of solidarity with Ukraine. The visit comes as Zelenskyy faces public difficulties with Donald Trump over US aid and support levels. Burnham has emphasized the UK's sustained commitment, telling Zelenskyy and Ukraine: "We are with you for as long as it takes." This represents the UK positioning itself as a counterweight to perceived US wavering on Ukraine, with Burnham calling on other Western partners to step up their support.

MI5 chief confirms Russian Brexit interference but says impact was minimal

MI5 Chief Ken McCallum has confirmed that Russia did attempt to interfere in the 2016 Brexit referendum, validating concerns raised by Prime Minister Andy Burnham. However, McCallum testified that there is no evidence suggesting this interference materially affected the referendum's result. The statement represents a more measured assessment than Burnham's initial assertion of Russian attempts to sway the vote, with McCallum arguing against overstating the scale or impact of the interference campaign.

Argentina escalates Falklands dispute with maritime claim and arrest requests targeting UK oil officials

Argentina has filed a legal action with an international maritime tribunal over UK oil exploration in Falkland Islands waters, marking an intensification of tensions over the territory's sovereignty. The UK has simultaneously appealed to Interpol to reject Argentine "red notice" requests for the arrest of individuals involved in the oil project, which the British government characterizes as politically motivated. Tensions over the Falklands have reached their highest level since the 1982 war between the countries. The dispute centers on competing claims to offshore oil resources near the territory.

The International Monetary Fund has revised its growth forecast for the UK upward, providing a more optimistic economic outlook as Prime Minister Andy Burnham prepares his first Budget. However, the IMF has cautioned that higher energy prices are expected to create headwinds for the economy in 2027, potentially offsetting some of the near-term growth benefits. The mixed signals—improved growth but rising input costs—will likely shape fiscal policy decisions in the upcoming Budget.

Just one in five new jobs since 2014 went to British nationals, Centre for Social Justice reports

Centre for Social Justice has a right-leaning perspective on welfare and immigration issues.

A landmark report from the Centre for Social Justice, a think tank focused on poverty and social issues, finds that of all additional payroll jobs created in Britain since 2014, only one in five went to British nationals—meaning four out of five new jobs went to foreign workers. The stark finding suggests a significant shift in the composition of employment growth over the past twelve years and is likely to inform debate around immigration policy and labor market access.

The Institute for Government is hosting an event on 21 October to examine Labour's record on public service delivery and the challenges facing Andy Burnham, who now leads the government. The discussion will cover progress made under Keir Starmer on NHS waiting times, school absenteeism, and knife and neighbourhood crime, which have all shown improvement. However, the event will also consider ongoing problems that could undermine Burnham's reform agenda and implications for John Healey's first budget and the upcoming spending review.
