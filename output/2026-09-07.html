Burnham's devolution push needs stronger local accountability, editorial warns

Editorial perspective advocating stronger accountability

As Prime Minister Andy Burnham seeks to make English devolution the centerpiece of his agenda by granting mayors unprecedented power and money, accountability mechanisms have eroded alongside devolution's expansion. The 2015 abolition of the Audit Commission—part of a Conservative "bonfire of the quangos"—coincided with a period of risky local government investments and asset sales that led to multiple council bankruptcies, including in Slough and Croydon. More recent examples of unchecked local power include the Teesworks development led by Conservative mayor Ben Houchen, where a 2024 government report criticized a "culture of excessive confidentiality." Without restored scrutiny and transparency requirements, Burnham's devolution plans risk repeating past failures by giving substantial resources and authority to local politicians without adequate oversight mechanisms.

Education minister pursues mainstream school model for special needs provision

Education minister Georgia Gould is visiting state schools to develop a blueprint for transforming England's overwhelmed special educational needs and disability (SEND) provision toward greater mainstream school inclusion. She toured Thomas Lord Audley secondary school in Colchester to study how it persuades local parents that mainstream settings can meet the needs of children with special needs, rather than requiring separate specialist provision. Gould faces time pressure as the system struggles with a surge in SEND applications, and she frames the inclusion model as a way to reduce the burden on parents who currently have to fight for support.

Labour's water company accountability scheme faces 'stitch-up' accusations

Labour's new framework for holding water companies accountable through "powerful consumer panels" that will allow public questioning of executives and obtain redress for sewage and water issues has drawn criticism from campaigners, who argue the process is rigged in favor of the utilities. Documents reveal water companies have been allowed to advise on how the Consumer Council for Water implements the new accountability structure, raising concerns about regulatory capture. The scheme aims to give customers in England and Wales stronger mechanisms to challenge company management but appears compromised by industry involvement in its design.

Far-right protesters block asylum seekers in Portsmouth as anti-migrant tensions flare

Hundreds of far-right activists, some dressed in black and wearing balaclavas, blocked roads in Portsmouth on 6-7 September after a small boat carrying 140 asylum seekers arrived in the city, attempting to prevent the arrivals from being transported for processing. The protest lasted until 3 a.m., when police removed the crowds with threats of arrest; reports indicate two police car windscreens were smashed during the confrontation. The government condemned the "thuggish and intimidating behaviour" of the protesters. This incident represents the first time such a large boat of asylum seekers has been brought to Portsmouth and the first occasion a large anti-migrant protest group has attempted to block newly arrived asylum seekers from processing, following similar unrest two days earlier at Dover.

Israeli strikes on Turkish airbase mark shift from diplomacy to direct military confrontation in Syria

Israeli warplanes struck the Abu al-Duhur airbase in Syria on 18 August 2026, in a direct challenge to Turkey's growing military presence in post-Assad Syria. The strike marks a critical shift in the longstanding Israeli-Turkish rivalry from diplomatic posturing to military confrontation. Turkey rapidly expanded its military and intelligence footprint across Syria following Assad's fall, positioning itself as a key player in the region's new security architecture—a presence Israel views as a direct threat to its strategic interests, particularly its ability to interdict Iranian arms shipments to Hezbollah and maintain freedom of action over Syrian airspace. Israeli planners are alarmed by the potential introduction of a Turkish air defense system that could constrain Israel's operational capabilities. Analysts previously dismissed Turkish-Israeli tensions as largely rhetorical, but this strike demonstrates the collapse of traditional buffer zones has placed the two countries' defense establishments on a direct collision course, ending the era of managing crises through diplomacy alone.
