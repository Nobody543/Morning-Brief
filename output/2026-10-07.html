UK Politics

Badenoch charts Conservative course: air defence spending and Reform rejection

Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch has ruled out any deal with Reform UK, stating she would rather call a fresh election. She has simultaneously unveiled a £10 billion air defence plan, an initiative that reflects broad cross-party agreement that the UK faces growing threats to its airspace and must strengthen its defences in a deteriorating security environment. The twin moves—rejecting coalition arrangements while embracing substantial defence investment—signal Badenoch's strategy as she attempts to rebuild Conservative support.

Conservative bids to win back Reform voters risk alienating moderate base, research warns

Analysis from the British Election Study Internet Panel suggests that Conservative efforts under Kemi Badenoch to win back voters who have switched to Reform UK are unlikely to succeed substantially and may backfire by alienating existing Conservative supporters. The research compares Conservative voter destinations since the 2019 election, finding that while the party has haemorrhaged votes to Reform, attempting to appeal directly to those voters by adopting similar positions risks damaging the wider moderate base that kept the Conservatives viable. The party remains in third place in most opinion polls despite talk of a 'Keminaissance' following Badenoch's leadership elevation, with little electoral recovery evident since May 2026.

Global Politics

West Bank tensions escalate sharply as settler violence and Palestinian uprising risk intensify

As Israeli settler violence against Palestinians in the West Bank has accelerated with apparent military acquiescence, alongside increasingly severe Israeli restrictions on Palestinian daily life, experts warn of rising potential for a new Palestinian uprising or intifada. Conditions in the West Bank today are dramatically worse than in either 1987 (when the first intifada began) or 2000 (when the second broke out), with Palestinians experiencing virtually no sense of political future.

Historically, the first intifada began as largely nonviolent and drew on decades of local organising and civil society; the second (2000–2005) became much more militarised under armed groups like Hamas. Analysis indicates that any sustained new uprising would require organised leadership and civil coordination to sustain momentum beyond initial protest.

Iran faces severe constraints on missile and drone rebuilding after 2025–26 strikes

Iranian missile and drone production faces severe long-term constraints after comprehensive Israeli and U.S. military strikes in 2025 and early 2026 systematically damaged both manufacturing facilities and defence supply chains. Since 2024, Iran has expended thousands of ballistic missiles, cruise missiles, and attack drones in retaliatory strikes against Israel and U.S. military bases, and Israeli and U.S. counterstrikes destroyed additional systems and launchers.

Though U.S. and Israeli officials have claimed Iran can no longer produce missiles and drones, Iran's rapid recovery through spring 2026 raised concerns among some Israeli officials that production could reach hundreds of missiles per month. In the near term, Iran can likely assemble new systems from prewar stockpiles of finished components—potentially hundreds or thousands—but rebuilding severely damaged industries for long-term production of improved or new systems remains a major challenge.

Laboratory worker dies of suspected pneumonic plague in Russia

A laboratory worker in Russia has died after a suspected case of pneumonic plague—the most virulent and rapidly fatal form of plague, caused by the bacterium Yersinia pestis. Pneumonic plague has an incubation period as short as 24 hours and causes severe respiratory symptoms including cough and breathing difficulty; untreated cases are fatal. Notably, it is the only form of plague that spreads person-to-person via respiratory droplets from coughing, typically requiring close contact. The case has prompted responses from Russia and international health authorities.
