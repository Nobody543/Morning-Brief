Nearly a million young people in the UK lack jobs, education or training—part of an emerging 'lost generation'

An estimated 981,000 young people in the UK are classified as NEET (not in education, employment or training), a figure former New Labour minister Alan Milburn is investigating and has termed a 'lost generation' and 'moral crisis'. The annual cost of supporting such young people is put at £125 billion—more than the UK spends on either education or defence. Guardian columnist John Harris has profiled real NEETs like 23-year-old Callum Atkinson from Rochdale, exploring the job-search barriers they face including AI-generated rejection emails.

Labour minister Andy Burnham's pledge to house every rough sleeper in England by Christmas faces serious challenges as rough sleeping numbers increased by nearly 1,000 between January and June 2026. Education secretary warns against treating secondary schools as 'early exam factories'

Education Secretary Lucy Powell has warned that schools' widespread practice of teaching GCSE subjects to younger pupils—as early as year 9—is turning secondary schools into "early exam factories" and increasing disengagement from education. Powell plans to include proposals to end this practice in an overhaul of England's national curriculum and says she will reduce the amount of GCSE content to make it easier to fit into the official timetable.

The NHS is deploying a £40 million scheme in England this winter aimed at preventing unnecessary hospital admissions among vulnerable patients. UK wasted £100m on unused Brexit border inspection posts

Five of seven Brexit border inspection facilities built in England and Wales never became operational, resulting in at least £100 million in wasted taxpayer money, according to reporting based on government disclosures. The facilities were abandoned as the Conservative government vacillated over whether to implement goods checks at the border post-Brexit. This waste is part of the broader £4.7 billion cost of Brexit cited by the National Audit Office, which included customs software transformation and other border systems.

Labour MP urges UK to ditch Palantir NHS contract next spring

Labour-aligned opinion piece

Ben Coleman, a Labour MP on the health and social care committee, argues that the UK should end its £330 million contract with US tech firm Palantir to run the NHS England federated data platform when it expires in March. Coleman contends that public trust in Palantir is low, its benefits are contested, and other tools could deliver comparable advantages. He warns against replacing Palantir with a single British firm, as that would not give the NHS the supplier independence it needs—the ability to switch vendors without losing the infrastructure it has built.

AI-generated complaints overwhelm UK public sector inboxes

MPs, local authorities, lawyers, schools and other public institutions are experiencing a surge in AI-generated correspondence—half the emails one Labour MP's office receives are now some form of AI, according to a caseworker with a decade of experience. Though volumes began as a trickle in 2024, they have grown into what workers describe as an 'onslaught,' making requests and complaints more complex and time-consuming to process despite AI's theoretical efficiency benefits.
