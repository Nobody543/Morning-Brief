Cheshire and Warrington schedules first mayoral election for May 2027 with Labour and Conservative candidates confirmed

On 6 May 2027, voters will elect the first mayor of Cheshire and Warrington, leading a new combined authority spanning Cheshire West and Chester, Cheshire East, and Warrington. Labour has nominated Dan Price, the current Police and Crime Commissioner for the area, while Conservative candidate Ben Fletcher, a former 2024 parliamentary candidate, now works as chief finance officer for an online retailer. The Green Party, Liberal Democrats, and Reform party have not yet announced candidates.

The combined authority, established in February 2026 after years of stalled negotiations beginning in 2015, will control an investment fund of £21.7m annually for 30 years, with the first instalment of £10.85m already allocated for 2026/27. The mayor will oversee devolved powers in skills, transport, planning, and economic development, serving a population of 1,014,900 and an area that generates £37 billion in annual Gross Value Added. The mayoral election was initially planned for May 2026 but was delayed to align with local council elections in Cheshire East and Cheshire West and Chester.

UK government plans 'radical change' in support for young people, signalling possible benefit cuts

Prime Minister Andy Burnham announced the government is targeting "quite radical change" in how young people are supported, with plans for tougher benefit rules alongside greater assistance with employment and mental health. The announcement came before the release of a second report by former minister Alan Milburn on reducing numbers of young people not in work, education or training (NEETs). Burnham framed the overhaul as "rethinking childhood" from early years onwards, indicating a comprehensive review but not ruling out benefit restrictions as part of the package.

Post-midterm election, the Trump administration faces a choice between continuing economic pressure on Iran through blockade and sanctions, or pursuing diplomatic talks. According to expert analysis, the immediate pressure is on Washington to define its approach. The Trump administration may favour a patient policy of economic squeeze—arguing there's no urgency since Iran needs time to rebuild arsenals or advance its nuclear programme following earlier losses. Alternatively, Trump might see military advantage and seek a decisive defeat to boost his political standing. However, a practical constraint will be U.S. munitions shortages.

Tehran, meanwhile, faces internal pressure to end a "no-war, no-peace" status quo that regime leaders view as unacceptable. The regime has some incentive to negotiate but distrusts U.S. and Israeli commitments, requiring guarantees the Trump administration may not be willing or able to give. Iran's leadership has hardened after recent deaths of top officials and believes it can weather further economic pain.

US and China propose AI confidence-building measures to reduce escalation risks

The United States and China are establishing dialogue mechanisms on artificial intelligence and agreed to form a bilateral communication channel for AI-related incidents, following discussions between U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and Chinese Vice Premier He Lifeng ahead of Xi Jinping's September 2026 visit to the White House. Rather than arms control, these measures function as confidence-building mechanisms—voluntary, non-binding efforts to increase transparency between the world's two leading AI powers and reduce risks of inadvertent escalation.

The agreements include establishing a U.S.-China Super Intelligence Dialogue to exchange views on risks and benefits, with the next exchange scheduled for November 2026. These measures do not restrain innovation or alter the underlying balance of AI power, but reflect both countries' recognition that managing strategic competition in emerging AI technologies requires communication channels despite their broader geopolitical rivalry.
