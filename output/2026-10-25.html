UK Politics

Burnham signals openness to free movement with EU in honest public debate

Prime Minister Andy Burnham says he wants a "factual" and "evidence-based debate" with the public about returning to free movement of people between Britain and Europe, part of broader plans to renegotiate the UK's relationship with the EU. Burnham has previously suggested the new deal could involve the UK going "all the way" and rejoining the EU, with negotiations ahead of an EU summit in November.

Chancellor Healey prepares budget with devolution package and multiple revenue sources

Chancellor John Healey is preparing next week's budget with a major devolution package giving local leaders greater spending power. The plan features a new £1.5bn borrowing facility to be developed with mayors over two years, fulfilling PM Burnham's pledge that local leaders in England will no longer have to "come cap in hand to Westminster" and aiming to unlock regional economic growth.

Meanwhile, Healey is considering four different ways to raise money and boost his fiscal headroom while avoiding an increase to income tax, according to BBC Verify's analysis of options on the table.

Budget to inject £1bn for social and affordable housing, bringing 10-year total above £40bn

Next week's budget will include £1bn in new funding for social and affordable housing, bringing the total committed to the 10-year housing programme to over £40bn.

UK Finance lobby accused of cherrypicking data to oppose bank tax

UK Finance, a banking lobby group representing banks including Lloyds, JP Morgan, and Revolut, has been accused by campaigners of misleading the government with selective data about tax competitiveness. The group commissioned a report claiming Chancellor Healey's planned budget tax rise would make London less competitive than rival financial hubs, but critics say the figures omit major competitor Paris, distorting the comparison ahead of next week's budget decisions.

Renters' Rights Act expands tenant freedoms but triggers landlord concerns on rents

The Renters' Rights Act has given tenants greater flexibility to leave a tenancy early, a change students are welcoming. However, student landlords warn the reforms pose challenges and have prompted concerns about potential rent increases as the market adjusts to new rules.

Healey's first budget promises 'breathing space' but faces test from global pressures

Chancellor John Healey is preparing his first budget, having promised 'breathing space' for households and small businesses as the government attempts to address the cost-of-living crisis. The Guardian notes that global events threaten to complicate his ambitions. The article captures the informal nature of Healey and Prime Minister Burnham's relationship—they meet Sunday nights for curry and political discussion at Downing Street—using the symbolism of an Indian takeaway as shorthand for ordinary treats increasingly beyond reach for many Britons.

BBC editorial perspective

BBC political editor Laura Kuenssberg assesses Andy Burnham's position as prime minister, noting that while he may be dreaming of hosting the Olympics in 2040, he must first navigate economically and politically challenging years in between. The analysis frames the current period as a critical test of his premiership.

UAE capital transformed Manchester—Arab Spring fears drove Gulf investment

The Guardian examines how Middle Eastern politics reshaped Manchester's landscape, tracing the phenomenon to 2011 when Egypt's dictator Hosni Mubarak was toppled during Arab Spring protests. The resulting power vacuum—with the Muslim Brotherhood's Mohamed Morsi becoming a sworn enemy of the UAE, whose rulers feared Islamist advance—prompted the Emirates to expand investment abroad. Emirati-linked firms' ownership of Manchester land has increased 39% in ten years.
