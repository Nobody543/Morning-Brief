Burnham seeks 'common ground' with Trump; pledges UK will tackle disinformation and lead on AI standards

PM Burnham told reporters he expects to find "common ground" with President Trump during their first bilateral meeting at the UN General Assembly in New York. Left-leaning outlet; analysis piece frames Trump skeptically

UK faces defining foreign policy challenges under new Burnham government

A UK in a Changing Europe analysis identifies major foreign policy challenges confronting the Burnham government as it inherits an era of profound international instability. The predecessor Starmer government dealt with an unpredictable American President, Middle East crises, need to rebuild European relations, and managing China as both trading partner and security risk. New Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband has signalled intention to prioritize climate crisis and international development, emphasizing values and international law. However, it remains unclear whether PM Burnham shares these liberal internationalist values or can pursue them in an increasingly competitive great-power environment.

US ends Operation Inherent Resolve in Iraq and Syria on September 30; concerns linger over ISIL resurgence

Operation Inherent Resolve—the international military campaign formed in October 2014 to combat the Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant (ISIL), the militant group that had seized territory across Iraq and Syria—is concluding on September 30 as remaining US troops complete their withdrawal from northern Iraq, following an earlier pullout from Syria in April. The operation's end reflects coalition success in degrading ISIL's territorial control, but also raises concerns about future stability: experts worry whether ISIL could reconstitute, and note heightened security risks for Kurdish communities after the US departure. The withdrawal also complicates Iraq's relationship with Iran-backed militias that continue to destabilize the country.
