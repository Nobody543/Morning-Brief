UK says Iran likely involved in RAF Fairford incident

Prime Minister Andy Burnham said Wednesday there are "strong indications" Iran was involved in a security incident over the weekend at RAF Fairford, a UK military base in Gloucestershire used by the US to launch strikes on Iran. Five men in their 20s were arrested on suspicion of terrorism and explosives offences after three suspicious vehicles were spotted near the base early Sunday; they have been released on bail pending investigation.

Iran has denied involvement, with Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi saying Burnham was "barking up the wrong tree." US officials including President Trump have suggested the incident may have foreign state backing, though investigators have not yet determined whether any foreign government was involved. RAF Fairford has been a focal point for protests against UK involvement in the 2026 conflict with Iran.

Institute for Government: Burnham should establish National Care Service this parliament

Advocating for government action on social care

A commentary from the Institute for Government argues that Prime Minister Andy Burnham's plan to establish a National Care Service should begin now rather than waiting until after the next election, as currently planned. Burnham has promised to provide older adults with free personal care (support with daily tasks like dressing and washing) funded through adjustments to the pension triple lock, and said the government would implement the service "in phases."

However, the Institute notes that while Burnham's vision is ambitious, it remains vague on key details—the free personal care would apply only to older adults, not working-age adults (who make up over 40% of those receiving publicly-funded long-term care), and residential care costs ("bed and board") would not be covered, potentially leaving some facing enormous expenses. The commentary argues that delaying implementation until 2029 or later risks leaving the social care sector in limbo for three more years, breaks a Labour manifesto commitment, and creates political risk if Labour loses the next election, potentially allowing an incoming government to abandon the promise entirely.

The piece suggests immediate establishment with initial functions like workforce planning, involvement in pay negotiations with care workers, standardising assessment processes, and spreading best practice would address real sector problems and still allow expansion in a future parliament.

UK scraps 'one in, one out' migrant exchange scheme with France

The UK government has ended the "one in, one out" pilot programme with France, under which migrants who crossed the Channel illegally would be returned to France in exchange for an equal number of asylum seekers approved through legal routes. Approximately 1,500 people have been removed to France since the scheme began just over a year ago, with roughly 1,087 deported and a similar number processed for transfer to the UK.

The government cited limited scale as the reason for abandonment, with Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood acknowledging operational challenges in ramping up removals. While Channel crossings have fallen sharply this year to around 19,000—down 42% from the same period last year—the scheme itself was deemed insufficient. The French government indicated it too was keen to exit the arrangement. The UK says it will continue deportation flights and draw lessons from the pilot to accelerate removals of those with no right to remain.

MI5 warns of Chinese academic espionage campaign targeting UK AI research

Britain's MI5 domestic security service issued an unprecedented public espionage alert on Wednesday naming the China General Technology Research Institute (CGTRI) as a Chinese state entity that has been funding academic research in the UK on artificial intelligence, cybersecurity, covert communications systems, and steganography. MI5 says the CGTRI has "very strong ties" to China's Ministry of State Security (MSS, Beijing's civilian intelligence service) and that its primary purpose is to fund research that directly improves the MSS's technical espionage capabilities.

More than 100 UK-based academics have contributed to CGTRI-funded projects. MI5 acknowledged that many academics and institutions likely engaged in good faith without knowing of CGTRI's intelligence links, but warned that those who continue collaborating now risk prosecution under Britain's National Security Act (2023). Security Minister Dan Jarvis wrote to all UK university leaders urging them to end relationships with the organisation.

China's embassy rejected the allegations as "imaginary and purely fabricated," saying it opposes "baseless allegations" and that the warning undermines educational and research cooperation. This alert represents the first time MI5 has independently published a public espionage alert and reflects broader tension between UK security concerns about Chinese state activities and the government's recent push to strengthen economic ties with Beijing.

Trump-Xi summit: Experts divided on whether talks yielded substance or stalled progress

nonpartisan expert analysis

President Trump hosted Chinese President Xi Jinping for a three-day Washington summit covering trade, AI, and Taiwan. A War on the Rocks roundtable of experts split sharply on its significance. On AI, the two sides agreed only to establish a dialogue and crisis notification mechanism—modest outcomes unsurprising given that Trump favours AI acceleration while Xi worries about maintaining control, and Beijing suspects U.S. safety rhetoric masks efforts to suppress Chinese competition.

According to one expert assessment, pageantry dominated the visit with little room for substantive negotiation, leaving major issues unresolved. Others emphasized the diplomatic symbolism of Trump treating Xi as an equal on the world stage. The competing expert takes signal genuine disagreement over whether the summit advanced U.S.-China relations or simply postponed harder decisions.
