Chancellor Healey faces tight fiscal constraints heading into first budget

Think-tank policy analysis (Institute for Government)

Chancellor John Healey delivers his first budget on 28 October facing significant fiscal pressures. Global energy price shocks from Middle East and Ukraine conflicts have eroded the government's buffer against fiscal rules from £24bn to a margin the Institute for Government analysis suggests should not fall much further. Borrowing costs are already high, and postponing difficult cuts risks compounding pressures closer to the next election.

Multiple spending crises are brewing by 2029/30—£900m in unfunded defence cuts, pressure from a £7bn cost of increasing defence spending from 2.7% to 3% of GDP, and £8–15bn in real-terms erosion from inflation alone—making it unrealistic to both protect public services and fund new priorities like adult social care without confronting trade-offs now. The analysis identifies rising energy bills (estimated at a 16% jump in January alone) and recommends targeted, time-limited household support rather than repeating the £35bn costs of 2022–23 blanket support. Healey is advised to set out a tax strategy with principles and priorities rather than ad-hoc measures.

Burnham should integrate NEET support into broader working-age benefits reform, not create separate system

Think-tank policy analysis (Institute for Government)

Alan Milburn's review of young people not in education, employment or training (NEETs) has correctly identified system failures—inadequate employment support within social security, overwhelmed mental health services, fragmented local accountability—but the Institute for Government warns against creating a separate benefits system targeting 16–24 year-olds. Creating a new system for a group comprising roughly one in ten health and disability benefit claimants would be a "time-consuming distraction" and would reintroduce complexity that could undermine local coordination and create problematic incentives around age 25 transitions. Instead, government should reform the health and disability benefits system as a whole to make it more personalised across all working-age adults, while giving local authorities flexibility to join-up employment support, education, and health services tailored to their populations.

Conservative/right-leaning outlet

The Nordic region's long tradition of neutrality has ended as countries prepare for potential conflict.
