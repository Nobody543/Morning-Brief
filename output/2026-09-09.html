UK defends Israeli settlement sanctions after chief rabbi warns of danger to British Jews

Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband has rejected concerns from Chief Rabbi Ephraim Mirvis that the government's new trade sanctions on illegal Israeli settlements could increase risks to British Jews' safety. Miliband argues the UK can confront the Netanyahu government's actions while fighting antisemitism, and Israeli counterparts have assured the UK that intelligence cooperation will continue unaffected.

The move signals a significant policy shift: UK analysts note that sanctions on Israel were once considered politically risky, but PM Andy Burnham is now framing them as an electoral asset, with polling showing majority support across most voter groups. The sanctions respond to escalating settler attacks on Palestinians and large-scale settlement expansion plans that would effectively partition the West Bank, backed by a 12-country coalition.
