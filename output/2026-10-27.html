Downing Street clarifies Burnham did not propose freedom of movement referendum

After fuelling speculation over the weekend about potentially returning to freedom of movement with the EU, Downing Street was forced to clarify the Prime Minister's position. Budget to scrap controversial carers' allowance "cliff-edge" penalty system

Chancellor John Healey will announce in Wednesday's budget the abolition of the controversial "cliff-edge" rule that penalized unpaid carers with thousands of pounds in deductions when their earnings exceeded the weekly limit. Healey will introduce a new digital taper system for carer's allowance to replace the punitive mechanism, which The Guardian reports has long been regarded as one of the UK benefit system's most feared and unfair administrative practices.

AI embedded across Home Office to enforce immigration rules, report reveals

According to research by the Open Rights Group, an advocacy organization critical of immigration enforcement, artificial intelligence has been embedded across the Home Office in near-total secrecy as part of what the report characterizes as reinforcing a "digital hostile environment" for immigration. Serbian student-backed opposition ends Vučić's 14-year political dominance

After 14 years in power with his Serbian Progressive Party (SNS), Aleksandar Vučić faced an unexpected political defeat in parliamentary elections, with student-led opposition movements emerging as a serious challenge to his dominance. Reports of election irregularities including voter intimidation and beatings at polling stations marked the vote. Pre-election tensions ran high, with authorities amassing riot police and groups of armed men gathering in Belgrade's city center, leading many to expect violence. The outcome marked a significant shift in Serbian politics, with Gen Z demonstrating electoral power against the established order.

Vučić, a political veteran who had outmaneuvered rivals throughout his tenure, now faces questions about his role in the country's political future. The incoming opposition leaders inherit a daunting array of political, economic, and institutional challenges as they prepare to take power.

Lula wins unprecedented fourth term in Brazil, halting regional rightward shift

Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva has won Brazil's presidential runoff on October 25, securing an unprecedented fourth non-consecutive term with 51.8% of valid votes to challenger Senator Flávio Bolsonaro's 48.2%. The victory is historically significant—it marks the first time since Brazil returned to direct elections in 1989 that a candidate who lost the first round has won a runoff. Lula's win blocks a rightward shift in Latin America that the Trump administration had backed; former President Jair Bolsonaro (Flávio's father, currently jailed) refused to concede and has called for annulment of the results. Markets were caught off guard, with Brazilian assets falling sharply. At age 80, Lula will be among the oldest sitting leaders in the Americas alongside Trump and Nicaragua's Ortega.

Boston Dynamics proceeds with $1.3M ICE robot dog deal despite 300 internal protests

Ars Technica AI focuses on technology ethics concerns; framing emphasizes employee opposition and use case tensions

Nearly 300 Boston Dynamics employees have petitioned the company to cancel a $1.3 million contract to supply four modified Spot robots to US Immigration and Customs Enforcement, but the company has decided to proceed with the deal. The robots, modified by Connecticut-based contractor Radeco and renamed "Cerberus," have been equipped with military-grade additions including a robotic arm, thermal camera, and sensors for detecting explosive materials. The contract was finalized in September 2026.

Boston Dynamics employees argue the deal risks reputational damage and association with ICE's immigration enforcement activities; the company counters that the Spot platform comes with strict ethical terms prohibiting weaponization, and that the robots will be used by ICE's Homeland Security Investigations unit, which focuses on transnational criminal matters rather than removal operations. Boston Dynamics has stated it would void warranties, terminate software licenses, and pursue legal action if ICE misuses the robots. The petition, sent to leadership in early September, included roughly 150 anonymous signatures; the company employs more than 1,500 people.
