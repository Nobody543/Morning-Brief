Burnham announces GB Grid body to take over electricity grid investment from private companies

Prime Minister Andy Burnham will announce at the Labour conference the creation of GB Grid, a government-backed body designed to invest in the electricity grid and compete with privately owned companies such as National Grid. Social care reform ambitious but faces implementation challenges and funding questions

Prime Minister Andy Burnham has signalled his intention to reform social care in England, but Sir Andrew Dilnot, who proposed social care reforms 15 years ago, has cautioned that creating a new NHS-style social care system would cost "very substantial" sums and could take decades to implement. Labour insiders, however, are optimistic that Burnham can successfully convince the public to back a major new social care system—something seven previous prime ministers have failed to deliver. Analysts believe Burnham has a "golden opportunity" to offer older people better protection in return for changes to the triple lock (the guarantee that the state pension rises by the highest of wage inflation, price inflation, or 2.5%), though any such reform would likely require either tax rises or holding back pension increases to fund the multibillion-pound service.

Avanti West Coast rail service to be renationalised in March 2027

Ministers have announced that Avanti West Coast, Britain's largest intercity rail operator, will be renationalised on 7 March 2027 when its current contract ends. The company has been one of the worst-performing rail firms in the country for delays and cancellations. Prime Minister Andy Burnham has hailed the renationalisation, and ministers are also considering publicly owning new trains as part of plans to reduce costs paid to private companies and improve rail infrastructure and passenger experience.

Research suggests mayors could help restore public trust in government, but delivery alone isn't enough

New research from the Institute for Government, More in Common and the University of York reveals a significant disconnect between the public's experience of local government services (which they rate positively for bin collections, schools, and libraries) and their trust in government to act in their interests. Mayors in devolved areas are trusted more than local or national government, partly because they're seen as putting local people ahead of party politics and delivering tangible policies like lower bus fares.

However, the research emphasises that mayors don't resolve deeper trust issues on their own. Public trust is shaped not just by service delivery but by perceptions that government has broken promises and failed to reflect local priorities. According to researchers Claire Ainsley and Akash Paun, rebuilding trust requires government at all levels to focus on clear, credible delivery against major priorities like managing the economy and immigration, combined with greater honesty about what government can realistically achieve. They also note that public understanding of what mayors actually do remains unclear, suggesting that as devolution expands under Prime Minister Andy Burnham, there's an opportunity to clarify mayoral responsibilities while they maintain their distinctive local voice.
