Labour splits over Rosebank oil field as drilling decision looms

The UK Labour government faces internal divisions over whether to approve drilling at Rosebank, Britain's largest undeveloped oil and gas field in the North Sea, which holds an estimated 300 million barrels of recoverable oil. About a dozen Labour MPs have publicly opposed the project during parliamentary debate, arguing it is incompatible with the UK's climate commitments and citing emissions concerns; one MP said it "fails on every test" and will not reduce energy bills or create meaningful jobs.

Meanwhile, senior Labour figures, the Scottish Labour leader, and major trade unions back the development, emphasizing energy security and economic benefits. Energy Secretary Miatta Fahnbulleh and Prime Minister Andy Burnham have not yet announced a final decision but indicated one will come "in due course," with privately expressed expectation among senior figures that approval is likely. The conflict reflects Labour's attempt to balance climate pledges against cost-of-living pressures and the support of voters and unions for domestic energy production.

Japan's military faces critical time constraint that could undermine Taiwan contingency response

According to strategist Christopher Denzel, Japan's greatest military vulnerability in a potential Taiwan crisis is not resources or doctrine but procedural constraints and decision-making timelines. Drawing on a 2022 wargame, Denzel explains that Japanese law restricted emergency access to private land for dispersing US forces to only after an armed attack occurred—but by then it would be too late, since the trigger that authorizes access is also the deadline for military action.

One participant discovered an earlier cabinet option within the same law that other experts had overlooked, revealing that apparently rigid constraints may contain procedural flexibility that planners haven't exploited. Denzel argues that instead of assuming Japan is inflexibly bound by legal constraints, bilateral planners should probe for procedural room to maneuver, rehearse viable solutions through exercises, and build shared understanding between Tokyo and Washington of decision and action timelines. In a crisis with China over Taiwan, US forces must scatter from concentrated bases before the first missile strike arrives, but Japan's cabinet response cannot be predicted or scripted—only prepared for through finding unused legal options that preserve flexibility for leaders in both capitals.

Google DeepMind uses AI to formally verify Signal encryption protocol

Google DeepMind has used an AI-assisted framework called AlphaProof Code to formally verify key parts of the Signal messaging protocol's encryption down to machine-code level. The process produced about 6.5 million lines of machine-checked mathematical proof in just four weeks—a task that historically took expert teams years to complete.

Formal verification mathematically proves that cryptographic code works correctly under all conditions; DeepMind's breakthrough represents the first time AI has helped devise such a proof for a widely deployed encryption protocol at this depth, and the first implementation-level verification by any method. The Signal protocol protects billions of private conversations in Signal, WhatsApp, and Google Messages. While the verification does not guarantee freedom from vulnerabilities in other parts of the codebase or in deployment, testing against intentionally flawed Signal versions confirmed the system can identify real security problems.

Spain's first general strike in 14 years over housing crisis paralyzes economy, clashes erupt

Millions of Spanish workers—unions reported about 4 million participants—staged a 24-hour nationwide general strike on November 11, the first general strike in 14 years, protesting soaring housing costs and wages unable to keep pace with affordability crisis. Called jointly by Spain's two largest unions, CCOO and UGT, the walkout disrupted transport, factories, and ports and resulted in clashes between police and protesters at various locations.

The strike, held less than three weeks before a snap election on November 29, was triggered by weeks of housing-related unrest, including an 87-year-old woman's death following eviction. It represents a transformation of housing from a policy debate into the central political fault line of Spain's election campaign, with union leaders calling for government intervention in the housing market and wage reform to allow workers to afford decent accommodation.
