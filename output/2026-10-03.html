Burnham signals EU rejoin bid: polling shows voter appetite but warns of 2016-style polarisation

pro-Labour leaning; Research-focused academic analysis

Prime Minister Andy Burnham has signalled his government will explore options for changing UK-EU relations, potentially opening the door to another EU referendum. A Messina Group megapoll found that nearly half of all voters support Burnham's preference to rejoin the EU after the next election, with a fresh referendum campaign in which Burnham advocated for re-entry potentially boosting Labour's vote share by as much as seven points. Some senior cabinet ministers concerned about reopening Brexit divisions are privately urging caution on the issue, but the findings have bolstered those in No. 10 pushing for EU renegotiation.

However, Anand Menon, director of UK in a Changing Europe, argues that while a proper informed debate would be welcome, the political dynamics risk repeating the low-quality polarisation of 2016. He notes the key tradeoffs are now well-understood—customs unions have limited economic impact, EEA-style arrangements mean outsourcing defence decisions, Swiss-style deals face EU opposition—yet the debate threatens to simplify into a binary Rejoin vs Remain Out choice. The costs of staying outside the EU are now clearer than in 2016 given tangible Brexit impacts on trade and investment, though Leave hasn't convincingly made the case for Brexit opportunities beyond niche sectors like financial services and new technologies. Welsh First Minister demands devolution parity with Scotland

Rhun ap Iorwerth, Wales's First Minister, has called on Prime Minister Andy Burnham to deliver a "new deal" granting Wales equal devolution powers to Scotland. Speaking at the Plaid Cymru annual conference in Llandudno, ap Iorwerth argued his government has "a clear mandate" to devolve policing, justice and welfare powers from Westminster to Cardiff Bay. Burnham plans to scrap pension triple lock before 2030 deadline

Prime Minister Andy Burnham announced at his party conference that the government will pass legislation to scrap the state pension triple lock during this parliamentary term, even though the guarantee is protected until 2030. The triple lock mechanism ensures the state pension rises by whichever is highest of three measures: 2.5% inflation, or wage growth. Burnham's move signals an intent to bind his successor and forestall any future reversal, though the actual policy change won't take effect until after the current election cycle.

Can Burnham prove Labour can drive real change? Columnist's challenge to caution

Left-leaning opinion column

Guardian columnist Andy Beckett argues that Andy Burnham's government has an opportunity to demonstrate that Labour can drive transformative change to the economy and society. Beckett notes that a persistent assumption—internalised across the political spectrum—holds that Labour governments cannot truly reshape the economy, redistribute wealth, or empower disadvantaged groups without upsetting powerful interests, from British elites to the US government to global markets. He frames this "conventional wisdom" as having constrained Labour governments from the 1920s through Keir Starmer's recent tenure, but argues that volatile political moments like the present may offer an opening for Burnham to prove sceptics wrong and expand what Labour can actually do. Closed Russian airspace strains US military mobility across Eurasia

Following Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine, the closure of Russian airspace to Western aircraft has forced long detours around the continent, with major implications for military operations and logistics across Eurasia. Western countries banned Russian aircraft and Russia reciprocated, eliminating the Trans-Siberian air corridor that previously carried roughly 30% of European flights to Asia and over 360,000 annual flights before 2022. For military mobility specifically, the longer flight paths reduce operational efficiency, increasing fuel burn and transit times—a Helsinki-Tokyo route that once took 9 hours now requires 13 hours. This constrains U.S. military flexibility in the region, where aviation could historically find alternative routes through economics and geography, but geopolitical division now imposes structural constraints.
