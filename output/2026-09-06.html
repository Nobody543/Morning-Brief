UK Politics

Farage denies listening to foreign donations discussion as Reform UK reels from undercover scandal

Nigel Farage has claimed he 'wasn't even listening' during a recorded meeting at the centre of an undercover investigation into whether Reform UK sought to evade laws on foreign donations. The scandal has damaged the party during its conference and cost him two close aides, with insiders calling for operational changes as the party aims for power. Farage maintained he knew donation law 'better than anybody,' though the legal status of the allegations remains complicated—foreign donations to UK political parties are prohibited, but exactly what constitutes a violation depends on context and intent. Farage also disclosed personal strain, telling the BBC his family are 'begging' him to leave his job.

Reform MP Zia Yusuf faces accusations of racism over comments questioning whether refugee is 'British'

Reform UK's home affairs spokesperson Zia Yusuf has drawn accusations of 'straightforward racism' from Labour after suggesting that Sagal Abdi-Wali—a former child refugee from Somalia who is now leader of Camden council—is not British. Abdi-Wali is reportedly Labour's frontrunner candidate for the Holborn and St Pancras byelection following Keir Starmer's resignation. The post has prompted criticism within Reform as well as from Labour.

Labour defence chair urges 3% defence spending commitment citing Argentine threat to Falklands

Tan Dhesi, Labour chair of the defence committee, is calling on Prime Minister Andy Burnham to commit immediately to raising defence spending to 3% of GDP by 2030, citing this week's threats to the Falkland Islands from Argentine President Javier Milei. Dhesi highlighted confusion around Burnham's defence spending plans—the PM appeared to reassure him the 2030 target would be met, but Downing Street later retreated from that position—making urgent clarity on the 3% target even more important.

Chancellor Healey signals austere first budget as Middle East conflict roils global markets

Chancellor John Healey has indicated the UK faces a tough October budget due to global economic uncertainty driven by the Middle East conflict. In an interview with the Financial Times, Healey said the Iran war is likely to heavily influence his first budget on 28 October. He wants to ensure the country has a 'buffer' against uncertainty as global bond yields have hit their highest level in 18 years, reflecting investor anxiety about the conflict's impact on oil markets and economic stability.

Chancellor risks undermining Burnham's radical vision as Labour grapples with economic reality

slight left-leaning perspective

Despite Andy Burnham's strong personal approval ratings six weeks into his premiership and Labour's relief that ousting Keir Starmer appears vindicated, MPs detect tension between the PM's ambitious economic vision and Chancellor John Healey's more cautious approach. Burnham has had an unusually smooth start—no major domestic crisis, Reform on the back foot—but underneath the optimism there is unease that his premiership has yet to grapple seriously with economic reality. The mismatch between Burnham's optimism and the fiscal constraints Healey faces, compounded by Middle East instability and bond market stress, suggests No 11 may be in a bind trying to reconcile the two.

Cabinet minister condemns masked anti-immigration protesters for disrupting Dover traffic

Business Secretary Jonathan Reynolds has condemned dozens of masked, black-clad protesters who blocked Dover roads on Saturday, calling such actions 'not something we would want on British streets.' While acknowledging that peaceful protest is 'an essential part of British life,' Reynolds distinguished this as 'dressing up in costumes' and disrupting people's lives rather than legitimate protest.

English Devolution Risks Being Messy and Unequal, Warns Guardian Analyst

opinion piece

A Guardian opinion piece argues that Prime Minister Andy Burnham's devolution push—transferring power from London to English regions—will be undermined by Whitehall's reluctance to hand over genuine money and authority. Rather than sweeping reform, the piece predicts piecemeal deals, with Cornwall potentially missing out on a promised new mayor structure. The UK's long history of centralized governance means reshaping political structures carries major execution and coordination risks that Burnham is glossing over.

Race Begins to Challenge Sadiq Khan for London Mayor in 2028

Conservative and Reform UK candidates are gearing up to challenge Sadiq Khan's re-election as London Mayor in 2028. Khan, who has won the role three times, has not yet confirmed he will stand again, saying he will decide next year. Both opposition parties are targeting Labour voters and building ground campaigns, with Khan's incumbent position still considered the favourite in what's being framed as one of Britain's most high-stakes local races.
