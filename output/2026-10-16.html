UK Politics

Chancellor Healey set to target banks' profits in Budget tax raid

Chancellor John Healey is poised to announce new taxes on Britain's banks in his 28 October budget. Senior banking executives told the BBC they expect to be "on the menu" for tax measures. According to The Guardian, the tax raid is intended to help offset damage to public finances from the war in the Middle East. The move comes as Healey is in Bangkok, where the International Monetary Fund praised the UK's progress toward balancing the books.

SOURCES — BBC Politics/The Guardian Politics

Reform UK faces backlash over racist remarks by party members in undercover ITV investigation

Reform UK, led by Nigel Farage, has faced renewed criticism after ITV News broadcast an undercover investigation documenting racist, violent, and sexist comments made by party councillors and candidates. The footage, gathered over a year, showed multiple party members making offensive remarks. Farage defended the party's vetting procedures, claiming "you can't vet for future comments." However, senior Reform officials warned that Farage was "protecting" a high-profile mayoral candidate accused of making racist comments about Labour MP David Lammy, including telling him to "go home to the Caribbean." The investigation also captured Sarah Pochin, one of two female Reform MPs, complaining about sexism within the party, saying other officials "are so fucking chauvinistic" and exclude women. The incidents highlight ongoing tensions within the party over recruitment standards and internal culture.

Former DUP leader Jeffrey Donaldson sentenced to 15 years for child sex abuse

Jeffrey Donaldson, the former leader of the Democratic Unionist Party (a major Northern Irish political party), has been sentenced to 15 years in prison. He was convicted of 18 counts of sex abuse, including rape, against two women when they were children.

SOURCES — BBC Politics

Labour draws hard line against Green party over Zionism motion

Opinion analysis included

The UK's Equality and Human Rights Commission has opened an investigation into the Green Party following a complaint about a motion describing "Zionism is racism." The party was reported to the watchdog by MPs and antisemitism campaigners. Prime Minister Andy Burnham has stated that Labour cannot partner with the Green party while it endorses this motion, effectively ruling Greens out of future coalition arrangements. Labour party chair Bridget Phillipson confirmed that unless local Green groups disown the motion, "Labour will bring any such local arrangement to an end," forcing elected Greens in councils like Sheffield and Lewes to choose between maintaining power or adhering to party members' positions. Columnist Gaby Hinsliff argues this represents a new phase where Labour and the Conservatives can exclude populist challengers from power-sharing.

The UK economy grew by 0.1% in August, exceeding analyst expectations of a slight contraction. Chancellor Healey described the surprise growth as a "sign of resilience" ahead of the government's upcoming Budget announcement.

Shevaun Haviland, director general of the British Chambers of Commerce, has called on Prime Minister Andy Burnham to make repairing the economic damage of Brexit his top priority as he reconsiders Labour's red lines on EU membership. Haviland argues that the UK's current trading relationship with the EU is bad for business and welcomed Burnham's reopening of the Brexit debate, even though it creates uncertainty.

France backs UK designation under EU's Made in Europe industrial protection rules

France has dropped its opposition to including the UK in the EU's "Made in Europe" legislation, provided Britain aligns with the EU's position on tariffs for Chinese electric vehicles. This marks a breakthrough for PM Burnham, as France joins Germany in supporting UK designation under the Industrial Accelerator Act—draft legislation designed to protect European manufacturers from low-cost Chinese competition.

SOURCES — The Guardian Politics

UK Defence Secretary Streeting seeks to cool US-UK tensions over Ukraine support during Washington visit

UK Defence Secretary Wes Streeting visited Washington to reinforce UK backing for Ukraine and attempt to ease tensions between Britain and the US following a row over Starlink restrictions. Streeting said he was "reassured directly" by his US counterpart Pete Hegseth that the Starlink system had been restored. In his speech, Streeting argued that supporting Ukraine serves long-term US interests and presents a business opportunity.

SOURCES — The Guardian Politics

UK threatens visa ban on Eritrea over asylum deportation row

Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood has threatened to block all Eritrean citizens from entering the UK unless the Eritrean government agrees to accept the return of rejected asylum seekers and criminals. Mahmood said she would not hesitate to impose such measures on other countries "refusing to co-operate." The Eritrean government's embassy responded by accusing Mahmood of launching a "perfunctory political gimmick," claiming the UK had failed to engage in "meaningful and appropriate" dialogue. The Guardian describes Eritrea as one of the world's most secretive and repressive states, with documented arbitrary detention and severe restrictions on freedom of expression and religion. The country, located in the Horn of Africa and bordering Sudan, Ethiopia and Djibouti, has not held elections in 33 years since gaining independence from Ethiopia in 1993.

Labour faces pressure to reject 'scrounger' welfare narrative as CSJ releases dependency report

Left-leaning opinion piece

A new report from Iain Duncan Smith's Centre for Social Justice claims to identify 3,400 local areas where more than half the working-age population rely on out-of-work benefits, framing them as "economic opiates" and creating an interactive postcode map labelled "welfare hotspots." Guardian columnist Frances Ryan argues this language perpetuates harmful "scrounger rhetoric" and that Prime Minister Andy Burnham has a chance to reintroduce compassion to welfare policy rather than accepting the conservative framing of mass dependency.

Global Politics

Ukraine abandons Trump's energy truce and strikes Russian refinery as Zelenskyy says he feels betrayed

Reuters and AP reporting reflects Ukrainian perspective on betrayal and ceasefire violation

Ukraine has resumed attacks on Russian energy infrastructure, striking a refinery after abandoning a ceasefire on energy targets that U.S. President Donald Trump announced on October 11. Trump claimed both sides had agreed to halt strikes on each other's energy infrastructure, but Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said he learned of the deal through media reports and was never consulted. Zelenskyy says he now feels betrayed by Trump, particularly after the U.S. president earlier eased sanctions on Russian diesel exports and blamed Ukraine for global fuel shortages. Russia continued striking Ukrainian energy facilities even after Trump's announcement, contradicting claims the ceasefire was holding. The truce was one of several diplomatic overtures Trump pursued, though neither Russia nor Ukraine formally confirmed acceptance before fighting resumed. Russia's sustained attacks on Ukrainian energy infrastructure throughout the war have been a strategic attempt to cripple the country's ability to support its military and civilian population heading into winter.

SOURCES — Reuters World/Associated Press World

White House ordered restriction on Starshield systems for Ukraine, Reuters reports

According to Reuters, an exclusive report reveals the order to limit Starshield systems for Ukraine came from inside the White House. Starshield is a military variant of SpaceX's Starlink satellite internet system, critical for secure military communications in contested environments. The restriction marks a significant limitation on US support for Ukraine's war effort.
