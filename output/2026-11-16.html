UK Politics

Milburn report and PM Burnham lay out competing visions for tackling youth unemployment crisis

ConservativeHome sceptical of Labour's approach to subsidised placements

Prime Minister Andy Burnham has pledged a "root-and-branch rethink" of childhood support policies to address a million 16–24-year-olds not in education, employment or training (NEET)—a figure driven by austerity, the Covid pandemic, AI's disruption of the jobs market, and cost-of-living pressures. He framed the crisis not as generational failure but as political, insisting young people "did not wake up and decide they did not want to work," and committed to government intervention starting in childhood, well before unemployment becomes a concern, with promises of more vocational training in schools.

Alan Milburn's long-awaited report, due this week, recommends a different approach: gradually reducing employment costs over five years and establishing a legal right to job support for young people, saying current support is too vague. It also proposes government-subsidized work placements—a proposal that signals tension within government, given Burnham's previous criticism of benefit cuts as "crude cuts." Conservative voices see potential in elements of the plan but question whether Labour will prioritize the right components. The ConservativeHome framing is sceptical of Labour's implementation and reliance on subsidised placements.

Labour backbench rebellion over Rosebank oilfield expected to reach 100 MPs

As many as 100 Labour MPs are expected to sign a private letter opposing the giant Rosebank oilfield development, with 75 already having signed. The letter is being delivered to Prime Minister Andy Burnham in the coming weeks. The rebellion reflects broader discontent among backbenchers over multiple government policies this week, including the EU reset discussions and welfare changes proposed in the Milburn recommendations. The 2024 cohort of Labour MPs is playing a significant role in the Rosebank opposition campaign.

Post-Brexit food checks create delays; UK to have voice but no vote in new EU food rules deal

The government is proposing a reset of post-Brexit relations to address friction over food regulations, with EU Minister Hamish Falconer insisting the UK would retain influence over policy. In practice, delays and inspection hold-ups are already affecting hauliers: perishable goods including Morrisons products are languishing at inspection points like the Eurotunnel terminal in Calais. The deal being negotiated would give Britain a voice in EU food policy discussions but crucially no voting power—a trade-off the government is framing as sufficient influence.

HMS Vengeance concludes record seven-month Trident patrol, raising maintenance concerns

The Trident nuclear submarine HMS Vengeance has returned to HM Naval Base Clyde at Faslane in Scotland after a record seven-month deployment, during which it spent 217 days submerged. The submarine began patrol on 12 April and returned on Sunday, met by Defence Secretary Wes Streeting. The extended deployment has prompted concerns about the pace of maintenance work on the Royal Navy's ageing Trident fleet and the strain placed on crew members by lengthy operational periods.

Trump heads to Asia seeking to reset momentum after domestic setbacks

President Trump departs for Asia to attend ASEAN and APEC summits in Manila and Shenzhen, aiming to shift focus away from Republicans' midterm election defeats and loss of Congress. The centrepiece is an unprecedented three-way nuclear talks summit with China's Xi Jinping and Russia's Putin to discuss stockpiles and Ukraine settlement prospects. Trump also plans to announce commercial deals and push Pax Silica, a U.S.-led coalition on technology export controls and critical minerals, to counter Chinese influence in the region.

However, his political position at home—including an air travel meltdown and incoming Democratic congressional majorities set to block his agenda—undercuts his negotiating leverage. Experts expect modest results rather than major breakthroughs.
