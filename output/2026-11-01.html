UK Politics

Starmer and Reeves call for closer UK ties to Europe as US partnership becomes less reliable

Former Prime Minister Keir Starmer has warned that European nations must draw closer together as the US becomes a less-reliable partner under Donald Trump, cautioning that this shift will not reset once Trump leaves office. In her first major intervention since stepping down as Chancellor, Rachel Reeves has gone further, saying Brexit has made the UK "poorer, weaker and less secure" and arguing Britain's future should be inside the EU. Both interventions signal former Labour leaders shifting toward more explicitly pro-European positioning after a period out of office, reflecting concerns about longer-term changes to the transatlantic relationship beyond the Trump presidency.

Chancellor John Healey has suggested younger workers might not receive the same minimum wage increases as others before the next election, raising questions about whether the government will break its commitment to universal minimum wage rises. The BBC's political editor Laura Kuenssberg flagged the remark as potentially at odds with government promises made around wage policy.

Chancellor says new offshore wind projects must lower bills or face veto

Chancellor John Healey has announced plans to veto new offshore wind projects if they will not reduce energy bills for consumers. He framed clean power as critical to breaking the grip of international gas prices, but insisted renewables expansion must occur "at the right pace, in the right places, with the grid to carry it." The move has raised concerns among green energy advocates that the government is retreating from its pro-renewables agenda, prioritizing immediate bill relief over longer-term decarbonization targets.

UK budget introduces world-first ringfencing of preventative spending to focus on long-term outcomes

Tucked into Chancellor John Healey's budget is a potentially significant structural change: new spending designated as "preventative"—where initial outlay produces future savings exceeding the upfront cost—will now be ringfenced and protected from raids for immediate needs. The move appeared only briefly in Healey's speech and one paragraph of the 166-page budget document, but proponents argue it could fundamentally reshape how government prioritizes spending and measures success, shifting focus from short-term political cycles to long-term outcomes. The mechanism could either revolutionize public spending discipline or create new battles over which programs qualify for ringfenced status.

Britain's housing crisis requires fundamental tax reforms, not budget adjustments

left-leaning editorial

Commentary in The Guardian argues that Chancellor John Healey's budget failed to tackle structural problems in the UK housing market, with critics across the political spectrum saying his approach was too cautious. The analysis contends that fixing the housing crisis requires reimagining the tax system to discourage treating property as wealth storage and to allow people to move home affordably, suggesting Healey's budget was more focused on short-term political gains than long-term economic solutions.

Economists argue growth-chasing is ineffective; focus should shift to wellbeing beyond GDP

left-leaning editorial

A Guardian podcast episode features economists Karel Williams and Sukhdev Johal arguing that successive UK governments' attempts to boost economic growth—through increased spending, cuts, deregulation, Brexit, and regional levelling-up policies—have repeatedly failed to improve living standards per person, suggesting that chasing growth alone is misguided. The conversation explores alternatives to growth-focused policy frameworks and examines what "life after growth" could look like for British communities, while noting the persistent inequality and stagnation the UK economy has experienced despite policy efforts across the political spectrum.

Global Politics

South Korea posts record 93.7% export surge in October, driven by global AI chip boom

South Korea's exports nearly doubled to $115.3 billion in October, marking the sharpest monthly jump in over 50 years, as global demand for AI-related investment powered massive increases in chip and computer shipments. Semiconductor exports jumped 259.4% year-on-year to $56.44 billion, and computer sales spiked 496.6% on soaring server demand for AI services. Exports to China rose 133.6%, to the US 185.8%, to Europe 56.4%, and to Southeast Asia 128.0%. The October surge was aided by more working days due to timing of the Chuseok holidays. Year-to-date exports through October totalled $930 billion; at this pace, South Korea will reach the milestone of $1 trillion in annual exports within November, becoming only the fourth country to achieve this (after the US, China, and Germany).

Iran and Qatar pursue diplomacy as Trump mulls fresh strikes after midterms

Iranian negotiators held talks in Doha with Qatari mediators on Saturday and Friday to discuss de-escalation and prevent renewed conflict in the Gulf following the US midterm elections. Qatar proposed a seven-day trust-building plan under which the US and Iran would take daily reciprocal steps before broader negotiations, but Iranian officials deemed it unsatisfactory. The talks are being coordinated with Washington; US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said Saturday that President Trump has not decided whether to launch new strikes but would prefer diplomatic resolution. Iran, which has threatened "geographically unrestricted" responses to any attack, has readied its armed forces along the Gulf coast and Strait of Hormuz. The backdrop: a ceasefire between the US, Israel, and Iran broke down on 28 February 2026, triggering a war that continues to simmer, with neither side wanting full-scale renewed conflict but deep mistrust blocking agreement.

Iran's Supreme Leader vows 'geographically unrestricted' response to any attack

Iran's Supreme Leader Ayatollah Mojtaba Khamenei issued a statement ordering the armed forces to be prepared for immediate response to any threats, saying "any level or type of threat must be met with an immediate, impactful, and geographically unrestricted response." He said military commanders have reported high readiness along the Gulf coast, in and around the Strait of Hormuz and Gulf of Oman, and on Iran's northwestern and southeastern borders. The statement came as regional tensions mount following the midterm elections, with Trump hinting at possible resumption of strikes on Iran.
