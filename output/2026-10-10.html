UK Politics

Labour wins Holborn and St Pancras by-election, taking seat back from Greens with 45% vote share

Sagal Abdi-Wali won the Holborn and St Pancras by-election for Labour with 45% of the vote, defeating Green Party leader Zack Polanski who came second on 33%. Labour's deputy leader said the result showed the party is winning back leftwing voters who became disillusioned under Keir Starmer's leadership. The result marks a significant shift: Labour is now polling 15 points above the Greens, a sharp reversal from earlier when the Greens had been ahead before Starmer's resignation as Prime Minister. Starmer's old north London seat remains Labour, suggesting the initial damage from his tenure is beginning to heal.

Conservative inheritance tax proposal criticised as 'myth-based policy making' by Institute for Government

Policy critique from think tank with strong emphasis on policy failures

Kemi Badenoch's flagship inheritance tax proposal, which she announced at Conservative conference, would allow homes passed to children to escape inheritance tax—designed to let families retain ancestral estates. However, the Institute for Government's Jill Rutter has published a detailed critique arguing the policy rests on a false premise: that adult children desperately want to inherit and occupy their parents' homes. In reality, 90% of properties already attract no inheritance tax, and most adult inheritors already own their own homes; average life expectancy in the 80s means most "children" inheriting are approaching retirement themselves.

Rutter also warns the policy would distort the housing market by incentivizing property owners to over-invest in ever-larger family homes as a tax shelter rather than downsizing—the opposite of what Badenoch promised in prior years when advocating stamp duty abolition to improve housing mobility. The policy would cost billions to solve a problem that doesn't exist and would divert capital away from productive economic investment into unproductive housing assets.

Liberal Democrats face leadership revolt as Treasury spokesperson resigns over Ed Davey's performance

Charlie Maynard, the Liberal Democrat Treasury spokesperson and senior frontbench figure, has resigned in an attempt to unseat Ed Davey as party leader. The resignation signals a growing internal revolt after months of frustration over concerns that the party is failing to cut through with voters as polling slips. Daisy Cooper, the party's deputy leader, responded by claiming that "the vast majority of our MPs support Ed... and are furious at this distraction," but the public resignation against a backdrop of declining polling suggests real tensions within the leadership.

Prime Minister Burnham has promised to restrict non-compete clauses in employment contracts, saying that restrictions on what workers can do after leaving their roles have "gone too far." The pledge suggests planned government action to limit restrictive covenants that prevent employees from working for competitors or setting up rival businesses within a specified period after departure, a measure aimed at increasing labour mobility and worker freedoms.

Milburn review calls for new pre-apprenticeship scheme to tackle youth unemployment gap

Alan Milburn's review of Britain's youth jobs crisis will recommend establishing a national pre-apprenticeship scheme in England to bridge the gap between school and work. The scheme would offer further education, training and work experience to young people, addressing what Milburn's earlier analysis suggested could become a severe challenge: youth unemployment potentially reaching 1.25 million by the early 2030s if action isn't taken. The review is responding to a dramatic decline in youth apprenticeship starts and represents an attempt to provide a structured pathway for young people currently falling out of the job market.

UK coastguard chief Virginia McVea faces resignation calls over disputed pay-cut claim data

Virginia McVea, chief executive of the Maritime and Coastguard Agency, is facing calls to resign after an Office for Statistics Regulation investigation found that her agency misrepresented evidence for a controversial plan to remove pay from rescue officers. McVea had claimed over 90% of coastguard rescue officers supported the proposal, citing an official survey, but the regulator concluded the poll data did not sufficiently back up that assertion. The episode raises questions about the use of unverified statistics to justify contentious policy changes.

Former ICC judge Navi Pillay wins 2026 Nobel Peace Prize; US sanctions ICC hours later

South African-born jurist Navi Pillay, a former judge at the International Criminal Court who specialises in international justice and human rights, has been awarded the 2026 Nobel Peace Prize for her work advancing accountability in that field. The honour arrived with extraordinary timing: within hours of the prize announcement, the US imposed sanctions on the ICC itself. The near-simultaneous occurrence of the prize award and the punitive measure underscores continuing US-ICC tension, particularly over the Court's investigation and prosecution activities.
